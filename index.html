          How the score was tested

          I took 2,800 YouTube videos from two Kaggle datasets ("YouTubers saying things" and "Mr-beast transcribed videos") labelled 1,499 of them as top 25% or bottom 25% of their own channel's views (adjusted for video age), then trained text models and tested them on videos they had never seen. Balanced accuracy: about 52% within a channel, 64% across channels from script and title alone, and 93% from subscriber count alone. So any tool claiming 85% or more from a script is mostly measuring channel size. Script Doctor scores craft instead (hook, pacing, payoff, clarity) because that's the part you control.

          India: no public dataset pairs Indian scripts with views, so I used Kaggle's "Trending YouTube Video Statistics" India file (13,794 videos, 510 channels, Nov 2017 – Jun 2018), labelled each video top or bottom 25% of its own channel (adjusted for days since upload), and trained on title, tags and description. On channels the model never saw: 61.5% balanced accuracy, falling to 57.5% once month names were removed (they were picking up timing, not craft). The benchmarks above are from that data and are several years old: treat them as direction, not rules.
